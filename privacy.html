This policy applies to:

It does not apply to Atlassian's own products or to other Marketplace apps. Atlassian's privacy notice governs Atlassian's processing of Jira data and the Forge platform.

3. Information the app processes

3.1 Jira content processed transiently

When Jira delivers an issue or comment event, the app reads the affected Jira issue and, for comment events, the affected comment through Atlassian's Jira REST API. It processes:

  • the issue summary;
  • the ADF description, including text nodes, link URLs, and inline, block, and embed card URLs;
  • the ADF comment body, including text nodes, link URLs, and inline, block, and embed card URLs;
  • issue, project, and comment identifiers needed to address the correct Jira record; and
  • the event type, timestamps, and identifiers needed for idempotency and audit correlation.

This content is processed in memory in the Forge runtime to detect and mask sensitive values. The app does not send issue text, comment bodies, link URLs, card URLs, or detected values to us or to any third party. The app does not operate an external backend.

When masking is enabled, the app writes the masked value back to the current Jira field through Atlassian's Jira REST API. The masked value becomes part of the Customer's Jira data and is subject to the Customer's Jira configuration, permissions, history, notifications, and retention.

3.2 Audit metadata stored by the app

The app stores audit records in Forge Custom Entities. The audit record is intentionally metadata-only. It may contain:

  • a record key and an event key derived with HMAC;
  • the event type;
  • the Jira issue ID, issue key, project ID, and comment ID;
  • the detection status (PENDING, REDACTED, FAILED, or DETECTED);
  • the HTTP status of the Jira write, when applicable;
  • the rule IDs and count of findings;
  • a 16-character salted HMAC fingerprint for each finding;
  • the finding location and ADF node type;
  • a boolean indicating whether the finding list was truncated;
  • a boolean indicating whether the field was recovered or changed before writeback;
  • a snapshot hash, record hash, and outcome hash derived with HMAC;
  • the app version and audit hash version;
  • record creation, completion, and expiry timestamps; and
  • a retention expiry used by Forge Custom Entity TTL and the daily cleanup job.

Audit records do not contain raw summaries, descriptions, comment bodies, detected values, full event payloads, or Jira change-history values. A fingerprint is a keyed digest, not the original value, but it can be used to correlate the same value across records. The tenant HMAC salt is stored separately in Forge secret storage and is never returned to the administrative UI.

3.3 Policy configuration

The app stores the Customer's policy configuration in Forge key-value storage:

  • whether scanning is enabled;
  • whether dry-run mode is enabled;
  • the scope mode and immutable project IDs;
  • enabled rule IDs;
  • retention days; and
  • the account ID of the administrator who last saved the policy, plus the save timestamp.

3.4 Administrative and support information

If a Customer administrator contacts us, we may receive the administrator's name, email address, Jira site URL, and the details the administrator chooses to include in the request. We use this information to respond to the request, investigate issues, and protect the app. Do not send raw secrets, issue text, or comment bodies in a support request.

3.5 Logs

The app is designed to write metadata-only logs. Its logger allowlists fields such as event name, issue ID, issue key, project ID, comment ID, event type, status, audit status, HTTP status, deleted/failed counts, a truncation flag, a retryable flag, a bounded reason code, app version, and error class name. It does not intentionally log issue text, comment bodies, detected values, or full event payloads.

Atlassian may independently collect Forge invocation metadata, platform logs, and audit information under Atlassian's privacy notice. The app does not control Atlassian's platform-level logging.

4. How the app uses information

The app uses information to:

  • detect and mask secrets and sensitive data in the current Jira field;
  • apply the Customer's project scope and rule policy;
  • create tamper-evident audit evidence;
  • verify audit-record integrity;
  • prevent duplicate writes and update loops;
  • enforce Marketplace licensing;
  • retain and delete audit data according to the Customer's settings;
  • provide support and respond to security reports; and
  • maintain the security and reliability of the app.

The app does not use Jira content for advertising, profiling, model training, or sale. The app does not make external network requests.

7. International transfers and data residency

The app is designed to store data in the Forge environment associated with the Customer's Jira site. When Atlassian supports the Customer's selected data-residency region for Forge storage and runtime, the app inherits that residency. The app does not route data to an independent region or external service.

Atlassian controls the locations and safeguards for Forge platform processing. Customers should review Atlassian's data-residency documentation for their site and product.

8. Retention and deletion

8.1 Audit records

The Customer selects an audit retention period of 30, 90, 180, or 365 days. The default is 90 days. The app records an expiry timestamp for each audit record and applies a Forge Custom Entity TTL. A daily scheduled job also deletes records whose signed expiry has passed and records older than the current retention setting, so the Customer is not dependent on eventual platform TTL cleanup and increasing retention does not extend older evidence.

The daily retention job continues to run when the Marketplace license is inactive. Deleting data on schedule is a privacy obligation, not a paid feature.

8.2 Policy configuration and tenant salt

The policy configuration remains until the Customer changes it or the app is uninstalled. The tenant HMAC salt is intentionally not deleted by the app's pre-uninstall hook: remaining audit hashes must still verify if uninstall cleanup is interrupted.

When the app is uninstalled, Atlassian's Forge lifecycle removes app storage. The app's pre-uninstall hook is best-effort: it attempts to delete up to 500 audit records, but Forge does not block uninstall on completion. Some records may remain until Atlassian's platform cleanup or TTL expiry.

8.3 Jira content

Jira content is controlled by the Customer and Atlassian. Masking changes the current field, but Jira may retain prior revisions, notifications, webhook deliveries, backups, and indexes under the Customer's Jira configuration and Atlassian's retention. The app cannot delete those copies. The Customer must use Jira administration tools and its own processes for any additional erasure.

8.4 Historical content

ZeroGuard processes issue and comment content when Jira delivers a create or update event after the app is installed. It does not automatically backfill or scan issues and comments that existed before installation. A Customer that needs a historical review must trigger edits or use a separate approved process; the app does not provide a bulk backfill feature in v1.0.

9. Security

The app uses:

  • Forge-hosted runtime and storage, subject to Atlassian's Forge security controls;
  • HMAC-SHA-256 with a per-tenant salt for fingerprints and audit hashes;
  • canonical JSON and domain separation for audit verification;
  • metadata-only audit records and logs;
  • backend-only storage access (the UI does not receive the tenant salt);
  • notifyUsers=false on Jira writes;
  • trigger ignoreSelf filtering to reduce update loops; and
  • idempotency and a pre-write snapshot comparison to reduce duplicate writes.

No method of transmission or storage is completely secure. The app is not a substitute for credential rotation, Jira permission design, or the Customer's incident-response process.
